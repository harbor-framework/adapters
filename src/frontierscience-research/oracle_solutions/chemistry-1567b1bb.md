# *meso*-Nitrogen modification of nickel(II) phthalocyanine: synthesis, π-electron count, aromaticity, spectroscopy, redox behaviour and reactivity

## 1. The limitation of traditional phthalocyanine synthesis, and why the *meso* positions are untouchable

Phthalocyanines (Pcs) are made, essentially without exception, by a **one-step condensation (cyclotetramerisation) of isoindoline-type precursors** — phthalonitrile, 1,3-diiminoisoindoline, diiminoisoindoline, phthalimide/urea mixtures or 1,2-dicyanobenzene — usually at 150–250 °C in a high-boiling alcohol or amine, with a metal salt or a strong base (Li/DBU/alkoxide) acting as template:

$$4\ \mathrm{o\text{-}C_6H_4(CN)_2} + \mathrm{M^{2+}} \xrightarrow{\ \Delta,\ \text{template, base}\ } \mathrm{M(Pc)}$$

The defining feature of this process — and the origin of its limitations — is that **all four *meso*-nitrogen (aza) bridges are created simultaneously, in a single, essentially uncontrollable step, as the four isoindole units assemble around the metal template.** Each *meso*-N originates from a nitrile carbon–nitrogen unit of the precursor, and the macrocyclisation is a cascade of nucleophilic additions and tautomerisations in which no intermediate (dimer, trimer, open-chain tetramer) can be isolated or manipulated. The consequences are:

- **No control over *meso*-position substitution patterns.** Because the four bridges are formed at once and all four must be nitrogen (they come from the nitrile nitrogens), it is impossible by this route to replace one *meso*-N by a carbon, to delete a *meso* bridge, or to install a substituent at a *meso* position. The *meso* positions are simply not addressable.
- **Statistical mixtures in unsymmetrical cases.** Condensation of two different phthalonitriles gives a statistical distribution of six constitutional isomers (A₄, A₃B, cis/trans-A₂B₂, AB₃, B₄), separable only with difficulty and in poor yield; even a single, 3-substituted phthalonitrile gives four regioisomers. Control of *where* the units end up relative to one another is therefore also poor.
- **No ring-size or ring-contraction control.** The thermodynamic sink is the 18π tetrameric macrocycle; contracted (corrole-, norcorrole-type) or expanded analogues are not accessible, so the π-electron count is fixed at 18.
- **Harsh conditions and intractable products.** High temperature, strongly basic or templating media and the extreme insolubility/aggregation of the planar products preclude late-stage functional-group manipulation.

This is in stark contrast to **porphyrins**, where the four *meso* positions are **carbons** that can be substituted at the monopyrrole stage (Lindsey/Adler condensations with aldehydes), or functionalised afterwards by electrophilic substitution, halogenation, cross-coupling, *meso*-amination, ring contraction or ring expansion. The entire structural diversity of porphyrinoid chemistry — corroles, norcorroles, N-confused, carbaporphyrins, expanded porphyrins, subporphyrins — rests on *meso*-carbon chemistry that the one-pot Pc condensation makes impossible. **The structural diversity of phthalocyanines has therefore been limited to peripheral (benzo-ring) and axial (metal) substitution, while the electronically decisive *meso* positions have remained synthetically inaccessible.** Overcoming this requires a fundamentally different, **stepwise** strategy in which an open-chain oligomer of defined length is prepared first and the final bridging atom is installed deliberately in a separate, controlled step.

## 2. The enabling methodology, step 1: thiolate-mediated reductive tetramerisation

The first element of the stepwise strategy is to stop the oligomerisation of the isoindoline precursor at the **tetramer** stage, before macrocyclisation.

**Thiolate-mediated reductive tetramerisation.** Treatment of the phthalonitrile-type precursor with an **alkali-metal thiolate (MSR, e.g. MSEt, M = Na, K, Cs)** does two things at once. (i) The thiolate is a strong, soft nucleophile that adds to a nitrile carbon, generating an anionic thioimidate/amidine-type adduct that is itself nucleophilic and attacks the nitrile of the next unit, so the units are strung together through **aza (amidine-type) linkages** — the same C–N connectivity that becomes a *meso*-N bridge. (ii) The thiolate is also a **single-electron reductant** (RS⁻ → RS• → RSSR), so the chain-growth step can also occur as a **reductive carbon–carbon coupling** of two radical-anion units, creating a **direct C–C linkage** between isoindole α-carbons. The result, under appropriately chosen conditions, is a **linear tetramer** comprising four benzo-fused pyrrole (isoindole) units — two *meso*-aza bridges and one central direct C–C bond — with the two **terminal units left at the sp³, 1,3-dihydroisoindole (isoindoline) oxidation level, i.e. bearing benzylic C–H protons at the two termini.** These terminal sp³ carbons are precisely the atoms that will become the fourth bridging position of the macrocycle, and they are the handle that makes *meso*-modification possible. The disulfide RSSR is the by-product of the reductive events.

**Selectivity control by the counter cation.** Whether the reaction stops at the open-chain tetramer or runs on to the closed macrocycle is governed by the **size of the alkali-metal counter cation**:

- With the **small, hard Na⁺**, the cation acts as an efficient **template**: it binds the growing anionic oligomer's imine/amidine nitrogens tightly in a small coordination sphere, pre-organises (curls) the chain so that the terminal and initial units are held adjacent, and thereby promotes **direct macrocyclisation** to the phthalocyanine (the classical Li/Na-templated Pc synthesis is the limiting case of this behaviour).
- With the **larger, softer K⁺ or Cs⁺**, the cation is a poor template: the metal–nitrogen interaction is weaker and more diffuse, the growing chain is not curled into a cyclisation-ready geometry, and the oligomer remains extended. Chain growth therefore terminates cleanly at the **linear tetramer**, which can be isolated, purified and characterised.

**Thus K⁺ or Cs⁺ thiolates give the open-chain tetramer selectively, whereas Na⁺ thiolate diverts the reaction to direct macrocyclisation (phthalocyanine).** This counter-cation control is the key to obtaining the tetrameric building block at all, and hence the key to the whole methodology; it also explains why the tetramer was never seen in classical Pc syntheses, which are run with small templating cations.

## 3. The enabling methodology, step 2: nickel complexation induces the folded conformation

The isolated tetramer, in its free (metal-free) form, adopts an **extended, essentially linear (zig-zag) conformation** in which the two terminal isoindoline units point away from each other. In this geometry the two terminal carbons are far apart (>10 Å) and cyclisation is geometrically impossible: attempts to close the ring on the free ligand fail.

**Complexation of nickel(II) solves this problem conformationally.** On treatment with a Ni(II) salt (e.g. Ni(OAc)₂, NiCl₂·6H₂O) and base, the four inner nitrogen donors of the tetramer chelate the metal in the square-planar fashion that Ni(II) ($d^8$) strongly prefers. Because the square-planar N₄ coordination geometry demands that all four donor nitrogens converge on a single metal centre at ~1.85–1.95 Å, **the chain is forced to curl from the linear into a folded (horseshoe/U-shaped, almost closed) conformation**, in which the two terminal isoindoline units are brought face to face and the two terminal (benzylic, sp³) carbons are **held in close proximity (~3 Å) and correctly oriented for bond formation.** The nickel therefore acts not merely as a Lewis-acidic metal centre but as a **conformational template/clamp** that pre-organises the tetramer for ring closure and converts an entropically and geometrically hopeless macrocyclisation into a fast, intramolecular, near-unimolecular process. **This conformational role of nickel — the linear → folded change on complexation — was confirmed directly by X-ray crystallography**, the structures of the free linear tetramer and of its folded nickel complex showing the two termini far apart and adjacent, respectively. Nickel is also ideal electronically: the small, square-planar, redox-innocent Ni(II) ion fits the contracted cavities that result from ring closure, which larger metals do not.

## 4. The enabling methodology, step 3: deprotonative functionalisation — one tetramer, three macrocycles

The folded nickel tetramer carries **two acidic benzylic C–H bonds** at the terminal sp³ carbons; they are benzylic *and* α to the imine/amidine π-system, hence doubly activated. Treatment with a strong base (LiHMDS, NaH, KO*t*Bu, LDA, 2 equiv) removes both protons to give a **dianion**:

$$\mathrm{Ni(tetramer)} + 2\ \mathrm{base} \longrightarrow \big[\mathrm{Ni(tetramer)}\big]^{2-} + 2\ \mathrm{base\text{-}H}$$

The dianion is a **doubly nucleophilic (bis-carbanionic/bis-enamide) species** whose two reactive carbons are held adjacent by the nickel-induced fold. Everything now depends on what is offered to those two carbanions: **the electrophile decides the identity of the fourth bridging position, and therefore decides which macrocycle is formed.** This single dianion is the common branch point for three structurally distinct ring systems.

### (a) Oxidants (PtCl₂ or NIS) → direct C–C bond → tetrabenzodiazanorcorrole (16π)

$\mathrm{PtCl_2}$ (a two-electron metal oxidant) or **NIS** (*N*-iodosuccinimide, an electrophilic iodinating/oxidising agent, which first iodinates one carbanion and is then displaced intramolecularly by the second, or simply effects two sequential one-electron oxidations) removes two electrons from the dianion and couples the two terminal carbons **directly to each other**:

$$\big[\mathrm{Ni(tetramer)}\big]^{2-} \xrightarrow[\text{(2 e}^- \text{ removed)}]{\mathrm{PtCl_2}\ \text{or}\ \mathrm{NIS}} \text{direct } \mathrm{C\!-\!C} \text{ bond} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Ni(tetrabenzodiazanorcorrole)}$$

Because the central link of the tetramer was already a direct C–C bond, the product has **two *meso*-aza bridges and two direct isoindole–isoindole bonds**: a **ring-contracted, norcorrole-type** macrocycle — a tetrabenzo-fused diazanorcorrole. No new atom is introduced; the macrocycle is contracted by the deletion of two *meso* bridges relative to a phthalocyanine.

### (b) Isoamyl nitrite → *meso*-N insertion → tetrabenzotriazacorrole (17π)

Isoamyl nitrite ($\mathrm{iAmO\!-\!N\!=\!O}$) is an electrophilic **nitrosating** agent (an "NO⁺" donor). One carbanion attacks the nitroso nitrogen to give a terminal C-nitroso/oxime-type intermediate; the second carbanion then attacks the same nitrogen intramolecularly, and loss of the oxygen (as alkoxide/water, i.e. dehydration of the intermediate nitroso/N-oxide adduct) closes the ring **through the nitrogen atom**:

$$\big[\mathrm{Ni(tetramer)}\big]^{2-} \xrightarrow[-\ \mathrm{iAmO^-},\ -\ \mathrm{H_2O/O}]{\mathrm{iAmONO}} \text{new } \mathrm{C\!=\!N\!-\!C}\ \textit{meso}\text{-aza bridge} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Ni(tetrabenzotriazacorrole)}$$

The product has **three *meso*-aza bridges plus the one direct C–C bond** inherited from the tetramer: a **corrole-type (singly contracted)** macrocycle, i.e. a tetrabenzotriazacorrole. Here the nitrite is a *meso*-nitrogen source, and this is precisely the controlled installation of a single *meso*-N that the classical condensation cannot do.

### (c) Benzoyl chloride → *meso*-C bridge → tetrabenzodiazacorrole (17π)

With an **acyl electrophile (benzoyl chloride)** the first carbanion is C-acylated to give a terminal aryl ketone; the second carbanion then adds intramolecularly to that ketone carbonyl, and dehydration (loss of the oxygen as water/hydroxide) aromatises the new bridge. The ring therefore closes **through a carbon atom** bearing the phenyl substituent:

$$\big[\mathrm{Ni(tetramer)}\big]^{2-} \xrightarrow[-\ \mathrm{Cl^-},\ -\ \mathrm{H_2O}]{\mathrm{PhC(O)Cl}} \text{new } \textit{meso}\text{-}\mathrm{C(Ph)} \text{ bridge} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Ni(}\textit{meso}\text{-phenyl tetrabenzodiazacorrole)}$$

The product is a corrole-type macrocycle with **two *meso*-N, one *meso*-C(Ph) and one direct C–C bond** — a tetrabenzodiazacorrole. This is the key demonstration of genuine ***meso*-nitrogen modification**: a nitrogen bridge of the parent phthalocyanine framework has been formally **replaced by a substituted carbon**, something no condensation route can achieve, and the *meso*-substituent (phenyl) is introduced by simple choice of the acyl chloride.

**Summary of the branch point:** one nickel tetramer dianion plus (i) PtCl₂/NIS, (ii) isoamyl nitrite or (iii) benzoyl chloride gives, respectively, the **16π diazanorcorrole**, the **17π triazacorrole** and the **17π diazacorrole** — three *meso*-modified tetrabenzoporphyrinoids with different bridge compositions, different ring sizes and, as shown next, radically different π-electron counts and properties.

## 5. π-electron counts and aromaticity (Hückel's rule)

The relevant quantity is the number of π electrons in the **main macrocyclic conjugation circuit** (the annulene-like pathway running around the inner ring), with the ligand in the charge state imposed by the square-planar, redox-innocent **Ni(II)** ion, which supplies two positive charges.

**Reference: phthalocyanine.** The $\mathrm{Pc^{2-}}$ ligand of $\mathrm{Ni(Pc)}$ has an **18π** main circuit. Hückel's rule for a monocyclic conjugated circuit states that

$$N_\pi = 4n+2 \Rightarrow \text{aromatic (diatropic)},\qquad N_\pi = 4n \Rightarrow \text{antiaromatic (paratropic)}$$

With $18 = 4(4)+2$, $n = 4$: Pc is **aromatic**, which is the origin of its stability and of its intense, sharp Q band.

**Tetrabenzodiazanorcorrole: 16π.** Deleting two *meso* bridges (replacing them with two direct C–C bonds) contracts the macrocycle and removes two π electrons from the main circuit relative to Pc. The dianionic norcorrole-type ligand bound to Ni(II) therefore has

$$N_\pi = \mathbf{16} = 4n\ (n = 4)$$

so by Hückel's rule it is a **4n, closed-shell antiaromatic** macrocycle: it sustains a **paratropic ring current**, has a small HOMO–LUMO gap, a destabilised HOMO and a strongly stabilised (low-lying) LUMO, and suffers antiaromatic destabilisation. This is the tetrabenzo analogue of nickel norcorrole, the classic stable 16π antiaromatic porphyrinoid.

**Tetrabenzotriazacorrole and tetrabenzodiazacorrole: 17π each.** The corrole-type (singly contracted) macrocycles require a **trianionic** ligand to be closed-shell 18π. Nickel is only divalent, so one electron is missing from the fully reduced 18π ligand: the ligand is formally **one-electron oxidised**, giving

$$N_\pi = \mathbf{17}$$

for both the triazacorrole and the diazacorrole complexes. Seventeen is **neither $4n+2$ nor $4n$**: these are **open-shell, $S = 1/2$ π-radical** macrocycles, lying exactly midway between the 16π antiaromatic and the 18π aromatic closed-shell states. They are therefore best described as **non-aromatic/weakly aromatic radicals** whose properties interpolate between the two closed-shell limits; they are not expected to show either a strong diatropic or a strong paratropic ring current, and their defining characteristic is radical (doublet) rather than ring-current behaviour. The odd electron sits in a macrocyclic π (SOMO) orbital, so a one-electron reduction gives the **18π aromatic ($4n+2$)** state and a one-electron oxidation gives the **16π antiaromatic ($4n$)** state — the three states are related by simple one-electron steps, which is the unifying theme of the spectroscopy and electrochemistry below.

**Relationship between π count and properties.** Reducing the π count from 18 (Pc) to 17 (corrole-type) to 16 (norcorrole-type) systematically (i) narrows the HOMO–LUMO gap, (ii) lowers the LUMO and raises the electron affinity, (iii) weakens or inverts the ring current (diatropic → none → paratropic), (iv) destroys the intense symmetry-allowed Q band of the aromatic 18π system, and (v) increases chemical reactivity, particularly toward nucleophiles and reductants. Each of these is observed, as set out below.

## 6. NMR spectroscopy: paratropic ring current versus paramagnetism

**16π tetrabenzodiazanorcorrole.** Its ¹H NMR spectrum is sharp (the molecule is diamagnetic, closed-shell, with Ni(II) $S = 0$) but the resonances are shifted **strongly upfield** of where an aromatic porphyrinoid would place them: benzo-ring protons that appear at δ 8–9 ppm in nickel phthalocyanine are found several ppm to lower frequency (δ ≈ 5–7 ppm and, for the protons closest to the ring, even further upfield). **Upfield shifts are the diagnostic signature of a paratropic ring current**, i.e. of **antiaromaticity**: in a 4n π system the induced macrocyclic current flows in the opposite sense to that in an aromatic ring, so the induced field *adds* to the applied field inside the ring and *opposes* it outside, deshielding internal and **shielding external (peripheral) protons** — exactly the inverse of the diatropic behaviour of phthalocyanine. The magnitude of the upfield shift is a measure of the paratropicity and, consistently, the computed NICS values at the macrocyclic centre are strongly positive and the anisotropy-of-the-induced-current-density plots show a clockwise (paratropic) circuit. This confirms the 16π, $4n$ assignment experimentally.

**17π triazacorrole and diazacorrole.** Their ¹H NMR spectra are, by contrast, **broad and poorly resolved (sometimes with no observable ligand resonances at all)**. The reason is different in kind: these species are **paramagnetic** ($S = 1/2$ ligand-centred radicals), so the unpaired electron produces large Fermi-contact and pseudo-contact shifts and, more importantly, very efficient nuclear relaxation through electron–nuclear dipolar coupling, broadening the lines beyond easy detection. The broadness therefore reports the **radical (open-shell) electronic structure**, not a ring current: in a 17π system the ring-current information is masked by paramagnetism, which is itself the clearest NMR evidence that the macrocycle carries an unpaired π electron. Variable-temperature NMR (Curie-law behaviour of the shifts) and the Evans-method magnetic moment ($\mu_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 1.7$–1.8 µ$_\mathrm{B}$, one unpaired electron) corroborate this.

Hence the NMR data map one-to-one onto the π counts: **sharp but upfield-shifted (16π, paratropic/antiaromatic) versus broad and paramagnetically perturbed (17π, open-shell radical)**, with phthalocyanine's sharp, downfield, diatropic spectrum (18π aromatic) as the third reference point.

## 7. ESR spectroscopy and the nature of the radical in the 17π systems

Both 17π macrocycles give well-defined, essentially isotropic ESR signals in solution with

$$g = \mathbf{2.0048}\quad\text{and}\quad g = \mathbf{2.0039}$$

for the tetrabenzotriazacorrole and tetrabenzodiazacorrole, respectively. The interpretation is unambiguous:

- These $g$-values lie **extremely close to the free-electron value $g_e = 2.0023$** (deviations of only $+0.0016$ to $+0.0025$), which is the hallmark of an **organic, ligand-centred π-radical**: in a light-atom π system spin–orbit coupling is tiny, so $\Delta g \approx 0$ and the signal is narrow and nearly isotropic.
- A **metal-centred radical would look completely different.** A $d^7$ Ni(III) or $d^9$ Ni(I) centre has large spin–orbit coupling and low-lying excited states, giving strongly **anisotropic** spectra with $g_\parallel$ and $g_\perp$ well separated and $g_{\mathrm{av}}$ typically $\geq 2.05$–2.2, often with resolved Ni hyperfine structure. Nothing of the kind is observed. **The radical is therefore ligand-based, and the nickel remains innocent, square-planar, diamagnetic Ni(II) ($d^8$, $S=0$).**
- The signals are narrow and show only small, poorly resolved hyperfine couplings to the macrocyclic nitrogens and benzo protons, indicating that **the unpaired electron is not localised on one atom or one isoindole unit but is delocalised over the whole macrocyclic π system.** That is exactly what the 17π description implies: the SOMO is a macrocyclic π orbital.
- This conclusion rests on **both experiment and computation**: the experimental $g$-values and hyperfine pattern are reproduced by DFT, which places the SOMO and the Mulliken/Löwdin **spin density delocalised over the macrocyclic π framework** (largest at the α- and *meso*-type positions, with only a few per cent on nickel), and the computed $g$-tensors are nearly isotropic and close to $g_e$. Experiment and theory therefore agree that these are **macrocycle-centred (organic) π-radicals of Ni(II) complexes, not Ni(III)/Ni(I) species**, confirming the 17π electron count.

## 8. UV-Vis-NIR absorption spectroscopy

**Reference: phthalocyanine (18π aromatic).** $\mathrm{Ni(Pc)}$ shows the classic Gouterman pattern: an extremely **intense, narrow Q band near 650–680 nm ($\varepsilon \approx 10^5\ \mathrm{M^{-1}cm^{-1}}$)** from the fully allowed $a_{1u}\!\rightarrow\!e_g$ transition of the aromatic 18π circuit, with a vibronic shoulder, plus a B (Soret) band near 330–350 nm, and essentially no absorption beyond ~750 nm.

**16π tetrabenzodiazanorcorrole.** Its spectrum is qualitatively different: **weak, broad and featureless absorption extending across the visible into the near-IR, with no intense Q band.** The reason is electronic-structural. In a $4n$ antiaromatic macrocycle the frontier orbitals are nearly degenerate (the HOMO is destabilised and the LUMO stabilised, giving a very small gap), and crucially the lowest-energy **HOMO→LUMO transition is symmetry-forbidden (orbitally forbidden) in the antiaromatic system**, so it appears only as a very weak, broad tail rather than as a sharp, intense band. The loss of the strong Q band, the appearance of weak low-energy absorption, and the broadness (short excited-state lifetimes, strong vibronic coupling in a near-degenerate system) are collectively the optical fingerprint of **antiaromaticity**, and they contrast point by point with the sharp, intense, high-symmetry-allowed Q band of the aromatic phthalocyanine.

**17π triazacorrole and diazacorrole.** These show (i) **Q-band-like absorptions in the visible** (reflecting the porphyrinoid π framework, though broadened and red-shifted relative to Pc and with lower molar absorptivity, because the circuit is contracted and open-shell) **and**, diagnostically, (ii) **additional low-energy absorptions reaching into the NIR-II window (~1000–1700 nm)**. Such very-low-energy transitions are **characteristic of radical species**: with a singly occupied molecular orbital lying between the filled and empty manifolds, new one-electron transitions (SOMO→LUMO and HOMO→SOMO, i.e. doublet–doublet transitions) become possible at energies far below the closed-shell HOMO–LUMO gap. The presence of NIR-II bands is therefore direct optical evidence for the 17π open-shell state, and it makes these macrocycles attractive as NIR-II absorbing dyes — a spectral region entirely inaccessible to normal phthalocyanines.

The three π counts thus give three distinct optical signatures: **intense, sharp visible Q band (18π aromatic); weak/broad, symmetry-forbidden absorption (16π antiaromatic); Q-like bands plus NIR-II radical absorptions (17π radical).**

## 9. Electrochemistry

**16π tetrabenzodiazanorcorrole.** Cyclic voltammetry shows **two reversible reduction waves** at remarkably positive (easily accessible) potentials, together with oxidation processes at higher potential. The two reductions correspond to the stepwise filling of the low-lying LUMO of the antiaromatic macrocycle:

$$\mathbf{16\pi\ (antiaromatic)} \xrightarrow{+e^-} \mathbf{17\pi\ (radical\ anion)} \xrightarrow{+e^-} \mathbf{18\pi\ (aromatic\ dianion)}$$

i.e. the first reduction converts the closed-shell antiaromatic compound into the **17π radical** state and the second gives the **18π aromatic** state. The ease of these reductions (high electron affinity, low-lying LUMO) is itself a thermodynamic manifestation of antiaromaticity: the molecule gains stabilisation by escaping the $4n$ count, so adding electrons is strongly favourable, and the reductions are chemically reversible because the resulting 17π and 18π species are the very same electronic structures that are isolable in the corrole-type series. The first reduction potential is far more positive than that of nickel phthalocyanine, confirming that *meso*-bridge deletion converts a modest electron acceptor into a very strong one.

**17π triazacorrole and diazacorrole.** These show a **narrow redox gap**: the first oxidation and the first reduction are separated by only a few hundred millivolts (markedly smaller than the ~1.5–1.7 V electrochemical gap of nickel phthalocyanine), and both processes are reversible. The reason is that the radical sits in the middle of a three-membered redox family and can be interconverted in either direction with little energetic cost:

$$\mathbf{16\pi} \underset{+e^-}{\overset{-e^-}{\rightleftharpoons}} \mathbf{17\pi} \underset{+e^-}{\overset{-e^-}{\rightleftharpoons}} \mathbf{18\pi}$$

Removing the odd electron from the SOMO gives the 16π antiaromatic cation-type state; adding one electron gives the closed-shell 18π aromatic state. Because the SOMO lies between the filled and empty manifolds, both steps are easy, and the **small HOMO–SOMO–LUMO separations translate directly into a narrow electrochemical gap and facile interconversion among the 16π, 17π and 18π states.** The small gap is simultaneously the electrochemical counterpart of the NIR-II optical absorptions (Section 8) and the reason these compounds behave as ambipolar, easily switchable redox systems — attractive for electron-accepting materials, redox switches and NIR-absorbing dyes. The redox patterns therefore map exactly onto the electronic structures: two reversible reductions marking 16π→17π→18π in the antiaromatic compound, and a narrow gap flanking the 17π radical in the corrole-type compounds.

## 10. Reactivity of the 16π antiaromatic macrocycle toward nucleophiles

The 16π tetrabenzodiazanorcorrole is **highly reactive, and specifically highly electrophilic**, in sharp contrast to the famous chemical robustness of phthalocyanines (which survive concentrated sulfuric acid and sublimation at 500 °C). Two factors act together: (i) the **antiaromatic destabilisation** of the $4n$ circuit, which any reaction that interrupts or expands that circuit relieves, and (ii) the **very low-lying LUMO** (large electron affinity, small HOMO–LUMO gap), which makes the macrocyclic carbons — particularly the α-carbons adjacent to the *meso*-aza bridges and the directly linked carbons — excellent acceptors for nucleophilic attack and lowers the barrier for addition. The macrocycle is therefore attacked readily by nucleophiles under mild conditions, and two transformations illustrate the point:

**(a) Hydroxide: ring opening.** $\mathrm{OH^-}$ adds to an electron-poor carbon of the contracted macrocycle (an α-carbon bearing/adjacent to a *meso*-aza bridge); the resulting alkoxide/amide intermediate collapses with **C–N bond cleavage of the *meso*-aza bridge**, so the macrocyclic circuit is **opened** to give a ring-opened (linear/partially opened) product with a terminal amide/imine function. The driving force is the destruction of the 16π antiaromatic circuit: once the cycle is broken, the paratropic current and the associated destabilisation disappear, and the product is an ordinary, non-aromatic conjugated oligomer. Nickel phthalocyanine, with its stabilising 18π aromatic circuit, is inert to the same treatment.

**(b) Hydrazine: ring expansion.** $\mathrm{N_2H_4}$ is both a good nucleophile and a bifunctional (bis-nucleophilic) reagent. It attacks an electrophilic macrocyclic carbon, and subsequent intramolecular attack by its second nitrogen on the adjacent carbon **inserts the N–N unit into the macrocycle**, so that the ring is **expanded** (a new, larger macrocycle containing an additional diaza/N–N bridging unit is produced, with loss of the contracted norcorrole framework). Again, the thermodynamic driving force is the **relief of antiaromatic destabilisation**: expanding the circuit changes the π-electron count away from $4n$ (toward an $18\pi$-type, $4n+2$ aromatic or at least non-antiaromatic circuit), so the paratropic ring current is quenched and the product is substantially stabilised.

In both cases, therefore, the reaction pattern is the direct chemical consequence of antiaromaticity: a 16π, $4n$ macrocycle with a low LUMO behaves as a potent electrophile, and nucleophiles convert it — by **ring opening (hydroxide)** or **ring expansion (hydrazine)** — into species whose π circuits are no longer $4n$, **relieving the antiaromatic destabilisation**. This reactivity is simultaneously a liability (the compounds are far less robust than phthalocyanines) and an opportunity, because it provides a controlled entry into ring-opened and ring-expanded tetrabenzoporphyrinoids that are themselves inaccessible by condensation chemistry.

| Reactivity | extremely robust | **electrophilic**: ring opening (OH⁻), ring expansion (N₂H₄) | radical reactivity, redox switching |
| Synthesis | one-pot condensation; no *meso* control | stepwise: thiolate tetramerisation (K⁺/Cs⁺) → Ni folding → dianion + PtCl₂/NIS | same tetramer dianion + iAmONO (N bridge) or PhCOCl (C bridge) |

Relative to the broader porphyrinoid family, these compounds are the **benzo-fused, aza-bridged counterparts of nickel norcorrole (16π antiaromatic) and of nickel corrole (a 17π ligand π-radical)**, demonstrating that the *meso*-aza bridges of a phthalocyanine can be deleted or exchanged for carbon in a controlled way, and that the porphyrin-like diversity of ring contraction, *meso*-substitution and variable π count can be transplanted into the phthalocyanine world. The benzo fusion and the aza bridges that remain keep the LUMO very low — so the 16π and 17π tetrabenzo systems are considerably better electron acceptors and absorb at considerably longer wavelength than their non-benzo-fused norcorrole/corrole analogues — while the stepwise route restores the structural control that phthalocyanine chemistry had always lacked.

## 12. Conclusions

1. **Traditional Pc synthesis is a one-step condensation in which all four *meso*-N bridges form simultaneously**, so substitution patterns at the *meso* positions cannot be controlled, and only peripheral/axial diversity has been accessible — unlike porphyrins with their addressable *meso* carbons.
2. **Thiolate-mediated reductive tetramerisation** stops the oligomerisation at an isolable linear tetramer; **the counter cation controls the selectivity — large K⁺ or Cs⁺ give the tetramer, while small Na⁺ templates direct macrocyclisation to the phthalocyanine.**
3. **Nickel(II) complexation converts the linear tetramer into a folded conformation**, bringing the terminal units together and making ring closure possible; the linear → folded change was **verified by X-ray crystallography**.
4. **Double deprotonation of the terminal benzylic positions gives a dianion** whose reaction with different electrophiles gives different macrocycles: **PtCl₂/NIS → direct C–C bond (tetrabenzodiazanorcorrole); isoamyl nitrite → new *meso*-aza bridge (tetrabenzotriazacorrole); benzoyl chloride → *meso*-carbon bridge (tetrabenzodiazacorrole).**
5. **π counts: 16π (diazanorcorrole, $4n$, antiaromatic), 17π (triazacorrole and diazacorrole, odd, open-shell radicals), 18π (phthalocyanine, $4n+2$, aromatic)** — Hückel's rule rationalising all the observed properties.
6. **NMR:** upfield shifts in the 16π system prove a paratropic ring current (antiaromaticity), whereas the 17π systems give broad signals because they are paramagnetic.
7. **ESR:** $g = 2.0048$ and $2.0039$, essentially $g_e$, identify **organic macrocyclic π-radicals rather than metal-centred (Ni(III)/Ni(I)) radicals**, with the spin delocalised over the macrocycle as supported by both experiment and DFT.
8. **Absorption:** weak, broad, symmetry-forbidden absorption for the 16π antiaromatic compound; Q-like bands plus NIR-II absorptions for the 17π radicals; both utterly unlike the intense, sharp Q band of phthalocyanine.
9. **Electrochemistry:** two reversible reductions of the 16π compound (16π→17π→18π), and narrow redox gaps for the 17π radicals from facile 16π/17π/18π interconversion.
10. **Reactivity:** the 16π system is highly reactive toward nucleophiles — **ring opening with hydroxide and ring expansion with hydrazine** — because both transformations **relieve the antiaromatic destabilisation** of the $4n$ circuit.

Taken together, the work shows that controlled modification of the *meso*-nitrogen atoms of nickel phthalocyanine — made possible by cation-controlled thiolate tetramerisation, nickel-induced folding and electrophile-selective deprotonative macrocyclisation — allows the π-electron count of the tetrabenzoporphyrinoid framework to be tuned from 18π through 17π to 16π, and with it the aromaticity, NMR ring currents, magnetism, absorption out to the NIR-II region, redox gaps and chemical reactivity. It converts the phthalocyanine skeleton from a structurally fixed, chemically inert 18π aromatic dye into a tunable platform for antiaromatic, open-shell and NIR-active functional materials.
