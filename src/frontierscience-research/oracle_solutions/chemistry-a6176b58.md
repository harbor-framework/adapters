# NS9283 analogues at the α4–α4 interface of LS (α4)₃(β2)₂ nAChR: synthesis, SAR and the NO₂ > CN effect

## 0. The common scaffold

All 26 analogues share a **3,5-disubstituted 1,2,4-oxadiazole** core, which in NS9283 carries a **pyridin-3-yl** group at C-3 and a **3-cyanophenyl** group at C-5:

**NS9283** = 5-(3-cyanophenyl)-3-(pyridin-3-yl)-1,2,4-oxadiazole, `N#Cc3cccc(c2nc(c1cccnc1)no2)c3`

The oxadiazole is a rigid, planar, weakly basic linker that fixes the two aryl rings at a defined angle (~1,3-relationship) and itself contributes two ring nitrogens and the ring oxygen as weak hydrogen-bond acceptors. Pharmacophorically the molecule is therefore a **planar, electron-poor, bis-aryl system with a hydrogen-bond acceptor (HBA) at each end**: the pyridine N at one end and the nitrile (or nitro, fluoro, ring N) at the other. This matches the small, flat, largely aromatic α4–α4 interfacial pocket, which is lined by aromatic residues of the α4(+) face (loops A–C) and the α4(–) complementary face and lacks the strong cation-recognition box of the orthosteric α4–β2 site.

## Part 1 — Synthesis route to the analogues

### 1.1 General strategy (amidoxime/acyl chloride route to 1,2,4-oxadiazoles)

Every compound in the series is assembled by the classical two-component **amidoxime + carboxylic acid derivative** cyclocondensation. The retrosynthesis cuts the oxadiazole into (i) a **nitrile → amidoxime** component, which becomes **C-3** of the oxadiazole, and (ii) a **carboxylic acid → acyl chloride** component, which becomes **C-5**.

**Step 1 — Amidoxime formation (installs the C-3 substituent).**

$$\mathrm{R^1CN} + \mathbf{NH_2OH\!\cdot\! HCl}\ (1.5\text{–}2.0\ \text{equiv}),\ \mathrm{Na_2CO_3/K_2CO_3\ or\ NaOH\ or\ Et_3N}\ (1.5\text{–}2\ \text{equiv}),\ \mathrm{EtOH/H_2O\ (or\ MeOH,\ DMSO)},\ 60\text{–}80\ ^\circ\mathrm{C},\ 4\text{–}16\ \mathrm{h} \longrightarrow \mathrm{R^1C(=\!NOH)NH_2}$$

For the NS9283 series the key nitrile is **nicotinonitrile (3-cyanopyridine)**, which with **hydroxylamine hydrochloride** (free-based in situ) gives **nicotinamidoxime** (*N*′-hydroxynicotinimidamide, `ON=C(N)c1cccnc1`). **Hydroxylamine hydrochloride and nicotinonitrile are thus the two starting materials for the amidoxime** that carries the pyridin-3-yl group of NS9283 and of the great majority of the analogues (Comp-1, 3–8, 10–15, 17, 18–22, and the C-5-varied 23–26 series retain this fragment or its close analogue).

Other nitriles are used in exactly the same way to vary the C-3 substituent:

| Nitrile (C-3 precursor) | Amidoxime | Appears in |
|---|---|---|
| nicotinonitrile (3-cyanopyridine) | nicotinamidoxime | NS9283, 1, 3–8, 10–15, 17, 18–22 |
| isonicotinonitrile (4-cyanopyridine) | isonicotinamidoxime | Comp-9 (pyridin-4-yl) |
| **cyclohexanecarbonitrile** | cyclohexanecarboxamidoxime | Comp-23 |
| **1-piperidinecarbonitrile** (*N*-cyanopiperidine) | piperidine-1-carboxamidoxime | Comp-24 |
| **furan-3-carbonitrile** | furan-3-carboxamidoxime | Comp-25 |
| methyl 3-cyanobenzoate | methyl 3-(amidoximo)benzoate | Comp-26 (→ saponification) |
| 4-fluoronicotinonitrile | 4-fluoronicotinamidoxime | Comp-16 |

**Step 2 — Acyl chloride formation (activates the C-5 component).**

$$\mathrm{R^2CO_2H} + \mathbf{SOCl_2}\ (2\text{–}5\ \text{equiv},\ \text{neat or in toluene/DCM, cat. DMF}),\ \text{reflux}\ 1\text{–}3\ \mathrm{h};\ \text{then evaporate} \longrightarrow \mathrm{R^2COCl}$$

**The acyl chlorides were synthesised using thionyl chloride** (oxalyl chloride, $\mathrm{(COCl)_2}$/DMF, or $\mathrm{PCl_5}$ are equivalent reagents; for the acid-sensitive *N*-acyl piperidine acids, oxalyl chloride at 0 °C or direct coupling with CDI/EDC·HCl is the practical alternative). Required acids:

- 3-cyanobenzoic acid (NS9283); 4-cyanobenzoic acid (Comp-10)
- benzoic acid (Comp-1)
- 2-, 3-, 4-nitrobenzoic acid (Comp-3, 4, 5)
- 3-fluoro-, 4-fluoro-, 2,4-difluoro-, 3,4,5-trifluoro-, 2,3,4,5-tetrafluorobenzoic acid (Comp-6, 11, 7, 12, 13)
- 4-fluoro-3-nitro- and 3-fluoro-4-nitrobenzoic acid (Comp-14, 15)
- nicotinic acid / pyridine-2-carboxylic acid (Comp-2, 8) and nicotinic acid for Comp-9/16-type pairings
- **mono-methyl isophthalate** (methyl 3-(chlorocarbonyl)benzoate) for Comp-17 (ester carried through, then hydrolysed)
- **cyclohex-1-ene-1-carboxylic acid** for Comp-22 (**cyclohexenyl** at C-5)
- *N*-formyl- and *N*-acetyl-**piperidine**-3-carboxylic acid (Comp-18, 19) and *N*-formyl- and *N*-acetylpiperidine-4-carboxylic acid (Comp-20, 21); these are obtained from nipecotic/isonipecotic acid by *N*-formylation (HCO₂H/Ac₂O, or ethyl formate) or *N*-acetylation (Ac₂O or AcCl, base)
- 3-cyanobenzoic acid again for the C-3-varied series Comp-23–26

**Step 3 — *O*-acylation of the amidoxime.**

$$\mathrm{R^1C(=\!NOH)NH_2} + \mathrm{R^2COCl},\ \mathrm{Et_3N\ or\ pyridine\ or\ DIPEA}\ (1.2\text{–}2\ \text{equiv}),\ \mathrm{DCM/THF/dioxane},\ 0\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}\rightarrow \text{rt},\ 1\text{–}4\ \mathrm{h} \longrightarrow \mathrm{R^1C(=\!NOC(O)R^2)NH_2}$$

(the *O*-acylamidoxime; chemoselective acylation occurs at the oxime oxygen).

**Step 4 — Cyclodehydration to the 1,2,4-oxadiazole.**

$$\text{O-acylamidoxime} \xrightarrow[\ \text{or TBAF (1 equiv), THF, rt;\ or\ }\mathrm{Bu_4NF},\ \text{or NaOAc/DMSO, or MW }150\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}\ ]{\text{toluene, xylene, pyridine or DMSO, }110\text{–}140\ ^\circ\mathrm{C},\ 2\text{–}12\ \mathrm{h}} \textbf{3-R}^1\textbf{-5-R}^2\textbf{-1,2,4-oxadiazole} + \mathrm{H_2O}$$

The reaction proceeds by intramolecular attack of the amidoxime NH₂ nitrogen on the ester carbonyl, ring closure and loss of water; it can be telescoped with Step 3 in one pot (acyl chloride, Et₃N, then heat), which is the most convenient way to make the whole library in parallel. Purification: aqueous work-up, then silica chromatography (EtOAc/heptane) or recrystallisation from EtOH; identity by ¹H/¹³C NMR, HRMS and HPLC purity >95 %.

**Step 5 — Post-cyclisation modification: saponification for the carboxylate analogues.**

The free acid/carboxylate compounds cannot be carried through Steps 2–4 as free acids (they would be activated by SOCl₂ and self-condense), so they are made as the **methyl esters and then hydrolysed**:

$$\text{methyl ester-oxadiazole} \xrightarrow[\ \mathrm{THF/MeOH/H_2O},\ \text{rt}\ \rightarrow 50\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}\ ]{\mathbf{ester\ saponification:}\ \mathrm{NaOH\ or\ LiOH\ or\ KOH}\ (2\text{–}3\ \text{equiv})} \text{carboxylate} \xrightarrow[\text{or isolate as the Na salt}]{\mathrm{HCl\ to\ pH\ 3\text{–}4}} \text{carboxylic acid}$$

This **ester saponification is the step that gives Comp-17** (`O=C([O-])c3cccc(c2nc(c1cccnc1)no2)c3`, the 3-carboxyphenyl/carboxylate analogue, isolated as the anion at assay pH) and likewise **Comp-26** (3-carboxyphenyl at C-3). Mild base and short reaction times are required because 1,2,4-oxadiazoles are susceptible to ring-opening by hydroxide; LiOH at room temperature is preferred.

### 1.2 Compound-specific notes

- **NS9283, Comp-1, 3–8, 10–16:** nicotinamidoxime (from nicotinonitrile + NH₂OH·HCl) + the appropriate substituted benzoyl chloride (from the acid + SOCl₂), then cyclodehydration. Regiochemistry (which ring ends up at C-3 vs C-5) is dictated simply by which partner is supplied as the nitrile and which as the acid; Comp-2 and Comp-9 are made by pairing nicotinamidoxime (or isonicotinamidoxime) with nicotinoyl chloride, giving the bis-azine analogues.
- **Comp-18–21 (*N*-acyl piperidines):** *N*-formyl/*N*-acetyl **piperidine**-3- or -4-carboxylic acid is activated (SOCl₂/oxalyl chloride or CDI) and condensed with nicotinamidoxime; cyclodehydration is performed under the mildest conditions (TBAF/THF, rt) to protect the formamide.
- **Comp-22:** **cyclohexenyl** at C-5 from cyclohex-1-ene-1-carbonyl chloride (SOCl₂ on the acid); the alkene survives the thermal cyclisation.
- **Comp-23–26 — use of cyclohexenyl/cyclohexyl, piperidine and furan substituents.** For this sub-series the ring at C-3 is replaced rather than the benzonitrile at C-5, by choosing the corresponding nitrile for the amidoxime step: **cyclohexanecarbonitrile → cyclohexyl (Comp-23)**, **1-piperidinecarbonitrile → piperidin-1-yl, i.e. the *N*-linked piperidine (Comp-24)**, **furan-3-carbonitrile → furan-3-yl (Comp-25)** and **methyl 3-cyanobenzoate → 3-(methoxycarbonyl)phenyl → 3-carboxyphenyl (Comp-26)**; each amidoxime is condensed with 3-cyanobenzoyl chloride (from 3-cyanobenzoic acid + SOCl₂) and cyclodehydrated. The closely related **cyclohexenyl** member of this saturated/heteroaromatic replacement set, **Comp-22**, is made the other way round, from cyclohex-1-ene-1-carbonyl chloride at C-5. The **cyclohexenyl, piperidine and furan substituents** of the Comp-22–26 set are thus all installed by the same amidoxime/acyl chloride chemistry, simply by swapping the nitrile or the acid.
- **Comp-26:** amidoxime from methyl 3-cyanobenzoate + 3-cyanobenzoyl chloride → oxadiazole → **saponification** (LiOH) to the 3-carboxyphenyl compound.
- **Comp-14/15:** fluoronitrobenzoyl chlorides; note the activated aryl fluoride can suffer SNAr with amine bases, so pyridine/DIPEA and low temperature are used in Step 3.

## Part 2 — SAR implications of the screening

Responses are normalised to 30 µM ACh alone (= 1.0); values > 1 indicate **positive allosteric potentiation** at the α4–α4 site of LS (α4)₃(β2)₂; values ≈ 1 or < 1 indicate loss of potentiation (and, below 1, incipient inhibition).

### 2.1 Reference points

| Compound | C-5 aryl substituent | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Comp-4** | 3-NO₂-phenyl | **3.40 ± 0.85** |
| **NS9283** | 3-CN-phenyl | **3.21 ± 0.55** |
| Comp-10 | 4-CN-phenyl | 2.67 ± 0.45 |
| Comp-5 | 4-NO₂-phenyl | 2.23 ± 0.21 |
| Comp-6 | 3-F-phenyl | 2.09 ± 0.14 |
| Comp-1 | H (phenyl) | 1.98 ± 0.12 |
| Comp-11 | 4-F-phenyl | 1.56 ± 0.12 |
| Comp-3 | 2-NO₂-phenyl | 1.40 ± 0.01 |

### 2.2 The 1,2,4-oxadiazole/pyridin-3-yl core is required

Every compound retaining the pyridin-3-yl–oxadiazole–aryl framework shows at least some potentiation (≥ 1.4), so the scaffold itself is the pharmacophore. The pyridine nitrogen is an essential HBA: moving it (Comp-9, pyridin-4-yl at C-5, 2.22) is tolerated but not improved, and replacing the distal phenyl with pyridin-2-yl (**Comp-8, 1.50**) is clearly detrimental — a ring nitrogen immediately adjacent to the oxadiazole suffers lone-pair/lone-pair electrostatic repulsion with the oxadiazole N/O, twists or desolvates poorly, and loses the productive HBA vector. Comp-2 (3,5-bis(pyridin-3-yl), 2.34) shows that a pyridine N can substitute for the nitrile as the distal HBA, but less effectively than CN or NO₂, because its acceptor strength is accompanied by a desolvation penalty and by reduced ring electron deficiency.

### 2.3 Substituent **position** on the distal ring: meta (3-position) ≫ para (4-position) > ortho (2-position)

This is the single clearest trend in the data set. **3-position (meta) substituents (CN, NO₂, F) are superior to their 4-position (para) analogues:**

- 3-CN (**NS9283, 3.21**) > 4-CN (**Comp-10, 2.67**)
- 3-NO₂ (**Comp-4, 3.40**) > 4-NO₂ (**Comp-5, 2.23**)
- 3-F (**Comp-6, 2.09**) > 4-F (**Comp-11, 1.56**)

The ordering is reproduced for three chemically different substituents, so it reflects geometry rather than electronics: the meta vector points the acceptor atom into a sub-pocket of the α4–α4 interface at the correct distance and angle for a hydrogen bond to a backbone NH / Thr–Ser hydroxyl / ordered-water network, whereas the para vector projects it along the long molecular axis, where it meets the pocket wall and gains nothing.

**Ortho (2-)substitution is the worst option** (**Comp-3**, 2-NO₂, **1.40**): an *ortho* group clashes sterically with the oxadiazole ring, forces a large aryl–oxadiazole dihedral, destroys the coplanarity that the flat interfacial slot requires, and simultaneously removes the HBA from any productive vector. Comp-7 (2,4-F₂, 1.64) and Comp-14 (2-NO₂/4-F pattern, 1.82) confirm that any *ortho* substituent is costly.

Unsubstituted phenyl (**Comp-1, 1.98**) is a useful baseline: substitution at the 3-position *adds* potentiation (3-F +0.1, 3-CN +1.2, 3-NO₂ +1.4), whereas substitution at the 4-position with the small, weakly accepting fluorine actually *subtracts* (Comp-11, 1.56 < 1.98).

### 2.4 Substituent **identity**: NO₂ > CN > F

At the optimal 3-position the potency order is

$$\mathbf{NO_2\ (3.40) > CN\ (3.21) > F\ (2.09) > H\ (1.98)}$$

i.e. the substituent potency order is **NO₂ > CN > F**, and the nitro and nitrile groups likewise both beat fluorine at the 4-position (NO₂ 2.23 and CN 2.67 > F 1.56). The trend tracks **electron-withdrawing strength and hydrogen-bond-acceptor capacity together**: $\sigma_m(\mathrm{NO_2}) = 0.71 > \sigma_m(\mathrm{CN}) = 0.56 > \sigma_m(\mathrm{F}) = 0.34$, and the HBA strength/denticity follows the same order (nitro: two oxygens, large negative electrostatic potential, bidentate; nitrile: one linear lone pair, moderate; fluorine: a very weak acceptor). Thus potency rises with EWG strength and/or H-bonding capacity — strong EWGs both polarise the aryl–oxadiazole π-system (strengthening π-stacking, cation–π and dipolar contacts with the aromatic box of the α4–α4 interface) and provide a better acceptor for the interfacial H-bond. Polyfluorination does **not** substitute for one strong acceptor: Comp-12 (3,4,5-F₃, 1.77) and Comp-13 (2,3,4,5-F₄, 1.99) remain at or below the plain-phenyl level, because many weak, diffuse C–F dipoles cannot replace a single well-placed strong HBA and the extra fluorines increase lipophilicity and desolvation cost. The mixed NO₂/F compounds (Comp-14, 1.82; Comp-15, 2.16) likewise underperform the mono-*meta*-NO₂ compound, since the added fluorine perturbs the ring electronics and, in Comp-14, re-introduces an *ortho*-type clash.

### 2.5 Aromatic rings are optimal; non-aromatic replacements need the HBA repositioned

Replacing either flanking aromatic ring with a saturated or partially saturated ring is generally deleterious, confirming that **flat, aromatic rings are optimal** for this interfacial slot (π-stacking with the aromatic residues lining the α4(+)/α4(–) faces and the shape complementarity of a planar ligand):

- **Comp-22** (cyclohexenyl at C-5, **0.96**) — loss of aromaticity and of the HBA abolishes potentiation entirely.
- **Comp-23** (cyclohexyl at C-3, **1.52**) — a saturated, non-planar, HBA-free ring in place of the pyridine is strongly detrimental.
- **Comp-24** (piperidin-1-yl at C-3, **0.82**) — the worst compound in the set: an *N*-linked, electron-donating, basic, non-planar amine both destroys the electron-poor character of the oxadiazole and places a positively charged (protonated) centre where an HBA is needed.
- **Comp-25** (furan-3-yl at C-3, **1.30**) — an aromatic but weakly basic, poorly accepting five-membered ring; aromatic yet unable to reproduce the pyridine N's hydrogen bond.

However, the **piperidine series Comp-18–21 shows that a non-aromatic ring can be rescued if the hydrogen-bond acceptor is repositioned**:

| Compound | Ring/linkage | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Comp-18 | *N*-formylpiperidin-**3**-yl | 0.89 ± 0.21 |
| Comp-19 | *N*-acetylpiperidin-**3**-yl | 0.90 ± 0.18 |
| **Comp-20** | *N*-formylpiperidin-**4**-yl | **2.57 ± 0.42** |
| **Comp-21** | *N*-acetylpiperidin-**4**-yl | **2.29 ± 0.50** |

The only difference within each pair is where the amide carbonyl (the HBA) sits relative to the oxadiazole. In the **3-substituted** piperidines the acyl group projects at an angle that cannot reach the interfacial acceptor site and instead clashes with the pocket wall — activity collapses to baseline (0.89–0.90, i.e. no potentiation). In the **4-substituted** piperidines the *N*-acyl carbonyl lies on the long axis, roughly where the pyridine/benzonitrile acceptor atom of NS9283 would be, restoring 2.3–2.6-fold potentiation. **Non-aromatic rings are therefore viable only with the HBA repositioned** (4- rather than 3-substitution), although still below the best aromatic compounds — a useful result, since the saturated ring improves solubility and sp³ character for lead optimisation.

### 2.6 Bulky and polar/charged groups reduce activity through steric clash and desolvation

Groups that are either too large or ionised at physiological pH are poorly tolerated, and the data give three explicit examples:

- **Comp-17** (3-carboxylate/carboxyphenyl, **1.01 ± 0.17**) — complete loss of potentiation. The carboxylate is both **bulkier and far more polar** than CN or NO₂; it is fully ionised at assay pH, carries a large desolvation penalty on entering the predominantly aromatic, poorly hydrated α4–α4 interface, and its two oxygens cannot be accommodated in the narrow sub-pocket that fits a linear nitrile. Comp-26 (3-carboxyphenyl at C-3, 1.37) is equally poor for the same reason.
- **Comp-18** (*N*-formylpiperidin-3-yl, **0.89 ± 0.21**) — the bulky, polar *N*-acyl piperidine, with its HBA misdirected, clashes sterically with the interface and drops below the ACh-alone baseline.
- **Comp-19** (*N*-acetylpiperidin-3-yl, **0.90 ± 0.18**) — the same clash, made marginally worse by the extra methyl of the acetyl group relative to the formyl group of Comp-18.

Together with Comp-24 (0.82) and Comp-22 (0.96), these compounds define the steric/polarity limit of the site: the α4–α4 interface accommodates a **small, flat, electron-poor, mono-substituted aromatic system with narrow HBA vectors**, and any group that adds volume out of the ligand plane or a formal charge is rejected.

### 2.7 Consolidated SAR model

1. **Core:** the planar 3,5-diaryl-1,2,4-oxadiazole is required; it is the conformational anchor and contributes weak acceptors.
2. **Proximal ring:** pyridin-3-yl is optimal (HBA at the correct vector); pyridin-4-yl is tolerated; furan-3-yl, cyclohexyl and piperidin-1-yl are progressively worse; aromaticity plus a well-placed basic N is what matters.
3. **Distal ring:** mono-substituted phenyl with a **strong EWG/HBA at the 3-position**; **3-NO₂ > 3-CN > 3-F > H**; *para* substitution loses ~0.5–1.2 units; *ortho* substitution is strongly detrimental; polyfluorination and ring-N at the 2-position are unproductive.
4. **Ring type:** aromatic is optimal; saturated rings require the HBA to be repositioned (4- not 3-substituted piperidine) to retain activity.
5. **Limits:** charged (carboxylate) or bulky polar (N-acyl piperidin-3-yl) groups abolish potentiation through steric clash and desolvation cost.
6. **Design direction:** keep the flat bis-aryl oxadiazole; explore *meta*-substituents of NO₂-like strength and acceptor denticity (e.g. SO₂Me, SO₂NH₂, CF₃SO₂, 3-NO₂/3-CN hybrids, 1,2,4-triazine N) while keeping the molecule small, neutral and planar.

## Part 3 — Why Comp-4 (3-NO₂) potentiates better than NS9283 (3-CN)

**Comp-4 (3.40 ± 0.85) > NS9283 (3.21 ± 0.55)**: at the optimal 3-position the nitro compound is the best of the whole series, and both the nitro and the nitrile compound greatly exceed 3-F (Comp-6, 2.09) and plain phenyl (Comp-1, 1.98), giving the overall ranking **NO₂ > CN > F > H**. The two molecules are isosteric except for the C-3 substituent, so the difference is attributable to the properties of NO₂ versus CN, and specifically to the **dual role of the nitro group — a stronger electron-withdrawing group *and* a better hydrogen-bond-accepting group:**

**(i) Stronger electron withdrawal.** NO₂ is a markedly stronger EWG than CN by every scale: $\sigma_m = 0.71$ vs $0.56$, $\sigma_p = 0.78$ vs $0.66$, and the nitro group withdraws both inductively ($-I$) and by resonance ($-M$) with two polarised N–O bonds, whereas the nitrile withdraws mainly through one $-I/-M$ axis. The consequence is a more **electron-deficient, more strongly polarised aryl–oxadiazole π-system** with a lower-lying π* manifold and a larger molecular dipole. In the α4–α4 interface the ligand is sandwiched in an aromatic slot, so the binding is dominated by **π–π/π-stacking, cation–π (with the conserved interfacial Lys/Arg) and dipolar/quadrupolar interactions**: a more electron-poor ring stacks more strongly against electron-rich aromatic side chains (the classic electron-poor/electron-rich stacking preference), and a larger dipole couples better to the local field of the interface. NO₂ therefore buys extra affinity and, because potentiation at this operational agonist site scales with occupancy and with the degree to which the ligand stabilises the activated conformation of the α4–α4 interface, extra efficacy in the ACh-normalised response.

**(ii) Better hydrogen bonding with receptor residues.** The nitro group is a **bidentate hydrogen-bond acceptor**: two oxygen atoms each bearing substantial negative electrostatic potential, presented over a wide angular range, so it can accept one strong or two simultaneous hydrogen bonds from backbone amide NH groups, from Thr/Ser/Tyr hydroxyls on the complementary α4(–) face, and from ordered water molecules bridging to the protein. The nitrile, by contrast, offers a **single, strictly linear, sterically narrow lone pair** on nitrogen with a smaller acceptor surface, so it makes at most one hydrogen bond and only when the donor is aligned along the C≡N axis. The nitro group thus engages the interfacial hydrogen-bonding network more completely and more tolerantly of small geometric mismatch, while still being small enough (and, unlike the carboxylate of Comp-17, **neutral**, with no desolvation or charge-burial penalty) to fit the pocket.

**(iii) Why the two roles reinforce each other here.** Both effects act at the same position — the 3-position vector, which Section 2.3 shows is the one that points into the acceptor sub-pocket. Hence the strong EWG character and the superior H-bond acceptor capacity are delivered exactly where the receptor can exploit them, which is why NO₂ gains over CN at the 3-position (3.40 vs 3.21) and why the same substitution at the 2-position, where the vector is wrong and sterics dominate, is instead ruinous (Comp-3, 1.40): the nitro group's advantages are realised only when its geometry allows the hydrogen bond, which is itself evidence that H-bonding — not electronics alone — is part of the explanation.

**Summary.** Comp-4 outperforms NS9283 because the nitro group plays a **dual role at the 3-position**: it is the stronger electron-withdrawing group, making the aryl–oxadiazole system more electron-poor and more strongly polarised for stacking and dipolar contacts in the aromatic α4–α4 slot, and it is simultaneously the better, bidentate hydrogen-bond acceptor for the hydrogen-bonding residues (backbone NH, Thr/Ser/Tyr hydroxyls) and ordered waters of that interface. The same NO₂ > CN > F > H ordering recurs throughout the series, confirming the interpretation. The nitro group is nevertheless a chemically less desirable group for a drug candidate (metabolic reduction to nitroso/hydroxylamine/aniline, mutagenicity risk) — which is why the nitrile is retained in NS9283 itself and why bioisosteric strong acceptors (sulfone, sulfonamide) are the rational next step.
