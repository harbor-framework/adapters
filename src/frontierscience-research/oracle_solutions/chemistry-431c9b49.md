# Dissecting TC-2559 super-agonism at the (α4)₂(β2)₃ nAChR

---

## Part 1. A REST (replica-exchange with solute tempering) study of TC-2559 in the α4β2 binding site

### 1.1 Why REST

The question is whether TC-2559, inside the orthosteric pocket, behaves like a classical agonist — i.e. whether it makes the canonical cation–π/hydrogen-bond network with the aromatic box, drives loop C closure over the ligand, and couples that closure to the β1–β2 loop/Cys-loop/M2–M3 linker interface. These are *local* rearrangements with barriers of a few kcal mol⁻¹ that plain MD at 310 K samples poorly (pose metastability, loop C hysteresis, slow water reorganisation). REST2 (solute tempering) heats only the degrees of freedom of interest, so the cost scales with the size of the "hot" region rather than with the whole solvated membrane system, and the 310 K ensemble is recovered exactly by Hamiltonian replica exchange. Crucially, REST is an **unbiased, generalised-ensemble** method: no reaction coordinate has to be guessed in advance, which is appropriate at this stage because we do not yet know which coordinate distinguishes TC-2559 from ACh.

### 1.2 System setup

**(a) Source of the protein structures.** Both stoichiometries are needed, so I would take **experimentally resolved structures** wherever possible and fall back on homology modelling/prediction only for what is missing:

- Low-sensitivity **(α4)₃(β2)₂** and high-sensitivity **(α4)₂(β2)₃** α4β2 nAChR structures from the PDB — e.g. the X-ray structure of the HS (α4)₂(β2)₃ receptor with nicotine (PDB 5KXI) and the cryo-EM structures of the α4β2 receptor in both stoichiometries (e.g. 6CNJ/6CNK), used in their agonist-bound (activated/desensitised) and, where available, apo/resting forms.
- Where a construct differs from the human wild-type sequence (thermostabilising mutations, truncated MA–M4 region, engineered linkers), the wild-type sequence is restored and the model rebuilt by **homology modelling** (MODELLER/SWISS-MODEL) on the resolved template; for segments absent from every template (loops, the M3–M4 intracellular region) either a template-based loop build (MODELLER loop refinement, Rosetta) or an **AlphaFold2/AlphaFold-Multimer prediction** of the pentamer is used as the donor, with per-residue pLDDT inspected and low-confidence disordered stretches left out rather than modelled into spurious structure.
- Validation before simulation: sequence/alignment check of all five subunits and of subunit order around the ring (the interface identity is the whole point of the study), MolProbity/Ramachandran and clash analysis, disulfide connectivity (Cys-loop; the vicinal α4 Cys pair), glycosylation/ion sites discarded, protonation states assigned at pH 7.4 with PROPKA/PDB2PQR (histidines checked by hand), and a 100–200 ns unbiased equilibrium MD run per model to verify stability (Cα RMSD plateau, no interface separation, pore architecture preserved).

**(b) Ligand.** TC-2559 ((*E*)-*N*-methyl-5-[3-(5-isopropoxypyridin-3-yl)prop-2-enyl]pyridin-3-amine-type structure; the relevant feature is the protonatable secondary/tertiary amine) is built from its SMILES, protonated at the amine (dominant microspecies at pH 7.4, confirmed with Epik/ChemAxon p*K*a prediction), conformers generated, and parameterised with a consistent all-atom force field — CGenFF or GAFF2 with RESP/AM1-BCC charges (HF/6-31G\* ESP for the charged amine), with torsions around the propenyl linker refit to QM (B3LYP-D3/6-31G\*\*) relaxed scans to make sure the extended *E*-linker geometry is correct.

**(c) Poses.** TC-2559 is docked (Glide SP/XP or AutoDock Vina, with a rigid-receptor ensemble taken from the equilibration) into (i) both orthosteric α4(+)–β2(−) sites of each stoichiometry, and (ii) for comparison, the α4(+)–α4(−) site of (α4)₃(β2)₂ and the β2(+)–β2(−) interface of (α4)₂(β2)₃. Top-ranked poses are checked against the canonical pharmacophore (cationic amine in the aromatic box, cation–π to α4 TrpB/loop B tryptophan, H-bond to the backbone carbonyl of that tryptophan, contacts with loops A/B/C of the (+) subunit and loops D/E/F of the (−) subunit) and against the known SAR of TC-2559; 2–3 distinct starting poses per site are retained so that pose convergence can be demonstrated. ACh and nicotine are run as positive controls under identical conditions.

**(d) Environment.** The pentamer is embedded in a POPC (or POPC:cholesterol 3:1) bilayer with CHARMM-GUI, orientated with OPM/PPM; solvated with TIP3P water with ~20 Å of water above and below; neutralised and brought to 150 mM NaCl; CHARMM36m for protein/lipid; hydrogen mass repartitioning or 2 fs time step with LINCS/SETTLE; particle-mesh Ewald, 1.2 nm cut-offs; Nosé–Hoover/v-rescale thermostat at 310 K and semi-isotropic Parrinello–Rahman at 1 bar. Minimisation → six-step CHARMM-GUI restrained equilibration → ≥200 ns unrestrained equilibration per system before tempering is switched on. Simulations in GROMACS 2023 patched with **PLUMED 2.9** (`hrex` Hamiltonian-replica-exchange machinery).

### 1.3 REST2 protocol

**Hot (tempered) region — this is the key design choice.** The solute whose Hamiltonian is scaled comprises **the ligand together with the binding-site residues**: all residues with any atom within ~5 Å of the docked TC-2559, i.e. loops A, B and C of the (+) α4 subunit (including the aromatic box: TrpB, TyrA, Tyr/Ser of loop B, the loop C YxCC motif and the vicinal cysteines) and loops D, E and F of the (−) β2 subunit, plus any structural waters retained in the site. Side chains are always scaled; backbone atoms of the loop C tip and loop F are included as well so that loop closure/opening can be sampled, while the rest of the protein, the membrane and the solvent stay at the physical temperature (this is what keeps the replica number small and prevents the pentamer or the bilayer from melting). In PLUMED this is implemented by writing a processed topology in which the selected atoms' charges, Lennard-Jones ε and dihedral barriers are scaled by $\lambda_i = T_0/T_i$ (`partial_tempering` with the hot atoms flagged in the topology), one topology per replica.

**Temperature ladder and replicas.** The effective temperature range is **310 K to 1000 K**, distributed geometrically,

$$T_i = T_0\left(\frac{T_{\max}}{T_0}\right)^{i/(N-1)},\qquad T_0 = 310\ \text{K},\ T_{\max} = 1000\ \text{K}$$

with $\lambda_i = T_0/T_i$ (so $\lambda$ runs from 1.0 to 0.31). For a hot region of this size (a ligand plus ~40–60 residues) **12–16 replicas** give a uniform exchange-acceptance ratio of ~20–30 %; I would start with 16 and, after a 20 ns pilot, prune or re-space the ladder to equalise acceptance. Exchanges between neighbouring replicas are attempted every 1–2 ps (every 500–1000 steps); each replica is run for 300–500 ns (aggregate 5–8 µs per system), with the first 50 ns per replica discarded. Convergence diagnostics: flat acceptance profile, each replica performing a full round trip through the ladder (round-trip time and the replica-index "mixing" plot), and stability of all reported observables computed on growing halves of the data; two independent REST runs from different starting poses per site.

### 1.4 Analyses — ligand and protein behaviour

**Ligand behaviour (at the $T_0$ replica, which is the physical ensemble):**
- Ligand RMSD to the docked pose; occupancy/lifetime of the defining interactions: cation–π distance and angle between the protonated amine and the loop B tryptophan ring, the amine–TrpB backbone carbonyl hydrogen bond, π-stacking of the pyridine rings with loop C tyrosines, the isopropoxy group's contacts, and water-mediated bridges;
- a **cluster analysis** (RMSD-based GROMOS/hierarchical clustering, or k-means on ligand internal and pose coordinates) of ligand poses and of the pocket, to **extract the representative conformations** of each metastable binding mode and their populations; the dominant cluster is compared with the corresponding clusters obtained for ACh and nicotine to decide whether TC-2559 is "classical";
- ligand internal torsion distributions (propenyl linker) to see which bound conformer is selected and at what internal strain.

**Protein behaviour and receptor activation:** a set of **collective variables that describe receptor activation** is monitored and their free-energy surfaces computed:
- loop C closure (Cα–Cα or centre-of-mass distance between the loop C tip/vicinal Cys and the (−)-subunit loop E/β9 strand; equivalently the "capping" distance over the ligand);
- the ligand-binding-domain "blooming"/radial expansion and the inner–outer β-sheet relative twist, measured as the angle between the inner (β1–β2–β6) and outer sheets;
- the β1–β2 loop ↔ Cys-loop ↔ M2–M3 linker coupling distances (the activation-transduction triad) and the M2–M3 linker displacement;
- the pore profile at the hydrophobic gate (9′ Leu and 13′ Val Cα–Cα cross-distances and minimum pore radius from HOLE/CHAP), plus M2 tilt and the 9′ side-chain rotamer state;
- interface-specific contact maps (per-residue contact frequencies across α4(+)–β2(−), α4–α4, β2–β2).

**Free energies from the REST data.** Because REST2 is a generalised ensemble with overlapping Hamiltonians, the configurations from *all* replicas can be reweighted onto the 310 K ensemble and the free-energy surfaces of the chosen CVs obtained with **MBAR** (pymbar; the multistate reweighting of all replicas, which is the statistically optimal estimator here) or, equivalently, with **WHAM** over the replica ensembles:

$$F(\xi) = -k_\mathrm{B}T_0\ln\left[\sum_n w_n\,\delta\!\left(\xi - \xi(x_n)\right)\right]$$

with MBAR weights $w_n$ obtained from the self-consistent solution of the multistate free-energy equations. One- and two-dimensional FESs (e.g. loop C closure vs 9′ pore radius; loop C closure vs ligand RMSD) are reported with bootstrap/block-average error bars, for TC-2559, ACh and nicotine, at both stoichiometries. Cluster populations and interaction occupancies are likewise reweighted. No further biasing is applied at this stage: the whole point is that the REST ensemble itself, reweighted with MBAR/WHAM, yields the local FES around the binding site.

**Expected readout.** If TC-2559 gives the same aromatic-box interaction fingerprint, the same (or deeper) loop C closed-state minimum and the same coupling to the β1–β2/M2–M3 triad as ACh, then its behaviour in the orthosteric site is *classical* and cannot by itself explain a four-fold-greater maximal response.

## Part 2. If the orthosteric REST results are classical, what explains the high efficacy at the HS stoichiometry?

If TC-2559 inside the orthosteric α4(+)–β2(−) pocket behaves exactly like a classical agonist — same pose family, same interactions, same loop C energetics — then the extra efficacy cannot be encoded in the orthosteric site, whose structure is anyway identical in the two stoichiometries. The natural alternative is therefore that **TC-2559 occupies an additional, non-orthosteric binding site that exists only in the high-sensitivity (α4)₂(β2)₃ receptor, and that occupancy of this extra site potentiates gating**:

- The HS pentamer has the subunit order α4-β2-α4-β2-β2, so besides its two orthosteric α4(+)–β2(−) sites it uniquely contains a **β2(+)–β2(−) subunit interface**. The LS receptor instead has the α4(+)–α4(−) interface (the site exploited by NS9283 and responsible for the generally higher agonist efficacy of the LS receptor). Each stoichiometry thus possesses one "extra", stoichiometry-specific interface, and they are chemically different pockets.
- TC-2559 is known *not* to be able to access the α4(+)–α4(−) site, which is precisely why it is only a partial agonist at (α4)₃(β2)₂. The mirror-image proposal explains the HS data: TC-2559 *can* bind the **β2(+)–β2(−) interface** of (α4)₂(β2)₃, acting there as its own positive allosteric modulator (an "ago-PAM" acting in *cis*). Binding at this third, HS-only site adds extra free energy of closed→open isomerisation on top of the two orthosteric sites, raising the maximal open probability above that reached by ACh — i.e. **super-agonism arises from an additional binding site unique to the HS stoichiometry, not from anomalous behaviour at the orthosteric site**.
- Mechanistically this is plausible because the β2(+)–β2(−) interface is structurally homologous to an agonist site (it has the same loop A/B/C vs loop D/E/F architecture) but lacks the α4 aromatic box; a ligand binding there can still drive loop C capping and β-sheet twist in a third subunit, so three of five subunits contribute activation energy instead of two.
- Other formally possible explanations (a membrane-facing/transmembrane allosteric site, a second site in the vestibule, or altered desensitisation kinetics inflating the apparent maximum) should be tested, but the stoichiometry-specific nature of the effect — super-agonism exactly where the β2–β2 interface exists, partial agonism exactly where only α4–α4 is available — points to the extra-orthosteric-site model.

## Part 3. Interpretation of the β2 mutant efficacy data

| Mutant | Efficacy (rel. to ACh) | Affinity of TC-2559 |
|---|---|---|
| WT | ≈ 4 (super-agonist) | — |
| β2 D217A | 2.8 ± 0.8 | **unchanged** |
| β2 D218A | 1.5 ± 0.3 | decreased |
| β2 D217A-D218A | 1.1 ± 0.4 | decreased |
| β2 D195A | 0.93 ± 0.2 | decreased |
| β2 D196A | 1.1 ± 0.5 | decreased |
| β2 D195A-D196A | 0.52 ± 0.1 | decreased |

**(a) Where these residues are.** D195/D196 and D217/D218 are aspartates of the **complementary (−) face of β2** — they lie in the **loop F (F-loop, β8–β9 region) and the adjacent loop E/β-strand segment** that together with loop D form the (−) side of an agonist interface. Because β2 contributes a (−) face at *both* the orthosteric α4(+)–β2(−) interfaces *and* at the β2(+)–β2(−) interface of the HS receptor, **these mutated residues line the β2(+)–β2(−) interface**, i.e. exactly the extra, HS-specific pocket proposed in Part 2. Their chemistry fits: four aspartates presenting carboxylates to a **cationic** ligand such as protonated TC-2559 provide the electrostatic/salt-bridge and water-mediated recognition that a β2 (−) face lacks when it has no aromatic box. The result therefore says that **TC-2559 makes important interactions at the β2(+)–β2(−) interface, mediated by the loop F / loop E aspartate cluster**, and that these interactions are what convert it from a normal agonist into a super-agonist.

**(b) The affinity/efficacy dissociation is the informative part.**
- **β2 D217A is the clean case:** affinity for TC-2559 is *not* significantly changed, yet efficacy falls from ~4 to 2.8. A residue that alters efficacy without altering binding at the orthosteric site is not part of the orthosteric recognition surface; it is part of a *transduction/second-site* surface. This is the strongest single piece of evidence that D217 contributes to the non-orthosteric (β2–β2) site and to the extra gating energy it supplies.
- **D218A, and especially the D217A-D218A double mutant, abolish super-agonism** (efficacy 1.5 and 1.1, i.e. back to ACh-like), with reduced affinity. So the loop F aspartate pair is required *both* for high-affinity binding and for the supra-maximal response; removing both carboxylates converts TC-2559 into an ordinary full agonist — precisely what is expected if the extra site is lost while the two orthosteric sites still function.
- **D195A, D196A and the D195A-D196A double mutant go further:** efficacy drops to ≈1 for the singles and to **0.52 for the double**, i.e. TC-2559 becomes a *partial* agonist, and affinity is reduced in all three. Falling below the ACh response means these residues are not merely accessory to a potentiating site — they are needed for normal orthosteric activation as well, consistent with the loop F/loop E aspartates of the β2 (−) face participating in agonist binding and in the loop-F-mediated coupling of binding to gating at the α4(+)–β2(−) sites themselves.

**(c) The interpretational caveat that must be stated.** Because loop F belongs to β2, **every β2 mutation is simultaneously present in both kinds of interface**: in (α4)₂(β2)₃ there are three β2 subunits, two of which supply the (−) face of the orthosteric α4(+)–β2(−) sites and one pair of which forms the β2(+)–β2(−) interface. A mutation introduced by co-expressing mutant β2 subunits is therefore incorporated at the orthosteric *and* the β2–β2 interfaces at once, so an efficacy change cannot be assigned unambiguously to the second site, nor an affinity change unambiguously to the orthosteric site — the two effects are convolved, and the reduced affinities of D218A, D195A, D196A almost certainly report the orthosteric copies of the mutation while the efficacy losses may report either. The same ambiguity means that a loss of efficacy could in principle arise from impaired loop-F-dependent transduction at the orthosteric sites rather than from loss of a second binding site. Disentangling them requires **concatenated pentamer constructs** (linked α4-β2 dimers/trimers) in which the mutation is placed in only one defined β2 position — e.g. only in the β2 that donates the (−) face to the β2–β2 interface, or only in the orthosteric β2 copies — together with fixed-stoichiometry controls, radioligand binding at each site and, ideally, the α4(+)–α4(−) comparison in (α4)₃(β2)₂.

**(d) What the data tell us about TC-2559's function.** Taken with Part 2: TC-2559 is a **classical (orthosteric) agonist that additionally binds the HS-specific β2(+)–β2(−) interface, where the loop F/loop E aspartate cluster (D217/D218 and D195/D196) coordinates its cationic amine**. Occupancy of that third site supplies extra closed→open isomerisation energy, producing the ~4-fold supra-ACh maximal response; abolishing the aspartate cluster removes the extra site (and, for D195/D196, also degrades orthosteric activation), returning TC-2559 to full-agonist or even partial-agonist behaviour. Its partial agonism at (α4)₃(β2)₂ is the complementary consequence of being unable to use that receptor's α4(+)–α4(−) interface. Functionally, therefore, TC-2559 is an agonist with built-in, stoichiometry-selective positive allosteric modulation.

## Part 4. A computational method to elucidate the mechanism of super-agonism

Efficacy is, thermodynamically, the ligand's effect on the **free-energy difference between the closed (resting) and open (activated) conformations** of the channel:

$$\Delta\Delta G_{\text{gating}} = \left[G_{\text{open}} - G_{\text{closed}}\right]_{\text{ligand}} - \left[G_{\text{open}} - G_{\text{closed}}\right]_{\text{apo}}$$

So the method must **construct the free-energy landscape of channel gating in the presence and in the absence of TC-2559** (and of ACh as the reference), at both stoichiometries, and with the putative β2–β2 site either occupied or empty. Because the closed→open transition is a global, multi-µs, high-barrier conformational change, plain MD cannot do this: an **enhanced-sampling** free-energy method is required.

### 4.1 Proposed method: well-tempered metadynamics along data-derived collective variables

**Why this choice.** Metadynamics (or umbrella sampling / ABF along the same coordinate) gives the full FES along a chosen CV and allows direct comparison of the apo and ligand-bound landscapes. Its weakness is the CV: **defining a CV that captures the relevant degrees of freedom for a global conformational transition such as gating is not straightforward**, and a poor CV produces hysteresis and an unconverged landscape.

**Constructing the CV.** I would build the CV from data rather than intuition, by **dimensionality reduction on interatomic distances between the α-carbon atoms of the protein**:

1. Generate a reference pool of conformations spanning the transition: unbiased MD (several × 500 ns) started from the resting/closed and the activated/open (and desensitised) structures of both stoichiometries, plus, if needed, interpolated structures from targeted MD/ANM-based morphing between closed and open states (used only to *featurize*, never as data for the final FES).
2. Featurise each frame with the pairwise **Cα–Cα distances** of the gating-relevant subset (M2 helices and the 9′/13′ rings, the M2–M3 linkers, the Cys-loops and β1–β2 loops, loop C tips, and the inner/outer β-sheets of all five subunits), optionally transformed to contact-like functions $1/(1+e^{\kappa(r-r_0)})$ to bound them.
3. Apply **TICA** (time-lagged independent component analysis, with a lag time of 5–20 ns; deeptime/PyEMMA) — or **PCA** of the same distance matrix if a kinetic lag is not reliable — and retain the first two slow independent components, which for pLGICs typically correspond to pore expansion/9′ dewetting and to the LBD twist/M2–M3 coupling. Validate that the ICs separate the known closed, open and desensitised endpoints and that their autocorrelation times are the longest in the system.
4. Implement the retained ICs in PLUMED as explicit linear combinations of the selected distances (`DISTANCE` + `COMBINE`, or `PROJECTION_ON_AXIS`/`MATHEVAL`), optionally compressed into a **path CV** (`PATHMSD`/`ADAPTIVE_PATH`) joining the closed and open projections with a progression coordinate *s* and a distance-from-path coordinate *z*. Physically interpretable backups (minimum pore radius at 9′, number of waters in the hydrophobic gate, M2 tilt, β-sheet twist angle) are computed as spectator CVs for reweighting.

**Implementation details.**
- **Well-tempered metadynamics** in GROMACS+PLUMED on the membrane-embedded pentamer (same force field/membrane/salt setup as Part 1): bias factor γ = 20–40, initial Gaussian height 1.0–1.2 kJ mol⁻¹, widths σ set to ~⅓ of the equilibrium fluctuation of each CV, deposition every 1 ps, harmonic walls on *z* to keep the system near the path.
- **Multiple-walker** metadynamics (20–40 walkers sharing one bias), started from frames spread along the path, for an aggregate 20–50 µs per condition; or, if resources dictate, **umbrella sampling** with 40–60 windows along *s* (force constants tuned to give overlapping histograms) with WHAM/MBAR unbiasing, which converges more robustly and is trivially parallel.
- **Conditions compared:** apo, ACh-bound (2 orthosteric sites), TC-2559 in the 2 orthosteric sites only, and TC-2559 in the orthosteric sites **plus** the β2(+)–β2(−) site; each at (α4)₂(β2)₃ and (α4)₃(β2)₂; plus the key mutants (β2 D217A, D218A, D195A/D196A) in silico, to reproduce the experimental efficacy ranking.
- **Convergence and error:** time evolution of the FES and of $\Delta G_{\text{open}-\text{closed}}$, block analysis/bootstrap, reweighting to the unbiased ensemble (Tiwary–Parrinello or final-bias reweighting) to recover FESs along the physical spectator CVs, and demonstration of forward/backward hysteresis-free behaviour. A Markov-state-model analysis of adaptively seeded unbiased trajectories launched from the metadynamics ensemble is a useful independent cross-check, since it needs no CV at all and yields kinetics (opening/closing rates) as well as thermodynamics; replica-based schemes (OneOPES, bias-exchange metadynamics over several candidate CVs) are the natural fallback if a single CV proves insufficient, since they accelerate the relevant degrees of freedom without requiring one perfect coordinate.
- **Complementary free energies:** absolute binding free energy of TC-2559 at the β2(+)–β2(−) site vs the orthosteric site (double-decoupling/alchemical FEP, or funnel metadynamics), and alchemical mutation free energies to predict the D217A/D218A/D195A/D196A effects on binding — the combination of ΔΔG(binding) and ΔΔG(gating) is exactly the affinity/efficacy decomposition the mutagenesis data require.

**Expected outcome.** The deliverable is a set of gating FESs: in the HS receptor, TC-2559 with the third site occupied should stabilise the open-state basin relative to closed by ~RT·ln(4)-scale extra free energy compared with ACh (and reduce the barrier), while TC-2559 with only the orthosteric sites occupied should look ACh-like; in the LS receptor, TC-2559 should fail to lower the closed→open free-energy difference as much as ACh does, reproducing partial agonism. That pattern would establish the extra, HS-specific β2–β2 site as the mechanistic origin of super-agonism and identify, residue by residue, how its occupancy is transmitted through loop F, the β1–β2/Cys-loop/M2–M3 triad and the 9′ gate.
